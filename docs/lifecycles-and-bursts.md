# Step 6 — creator lifecycles and overlapping bursts

Implementation was separately approved after review of this design. This replaces the proposed changepoint experiment, not its historical evidence. The product supplies corroborating evidence for a user's memories, not an interpretation of their life. A statistically inferred bar is an inference about recorded actions, even when its inputs are counts; it must never be sold as a discovered personal truth.

Implementation conventions, recorded before the first real burst run: state zero is the prior state before the first bin; an elevated first bin pays its upward transition. Exact cost ties prefer lower summed intensity, then earlier upward boundaries, then lexicographic states. The date-only export endpoint cannot establish a complete final week, even when it is a Sunday: the entire endpoint week is conservatively unassessed. Lifecycle elapsed windows use the supplied export day's inclusive UTC endpoint, explicitly as a convention. Session histograms use fixed 0.25-wide log10-second bins; the weighted-component crossing and the mixture-density valley are reported separately. No support setting or grid value has changed.

The descriptive lifecycle stage was implemented and run independently with an explicitly labelled 60-minute session convention before session fitting or burst implementation. Reviewed derived settings then refresh session counts and their ranking tie-break only; calendar recurrence definitions do not depend on bursts. Sessions are grouped across each whole channel, with distinct session memberships counted per creator. The implementation accepts IANA timezones for local dates/calendar windows; UTC weeks stay explicit. Full personal records remain outside Git.

## What the two observation windows can support

- **Recent saved/folder window:** 2025-12-01 through the supplied 2026-08-30 export date. Of 73 retained folders, 67 were created in 2026 (30 in January); 4,047/4,809 saves are from 2026, while 90.96% of 2025 saves lack a matched placement. Current folders provide dense labelling roughly December 2025–August 2026, not historical topic labelling across eight years. There are two earlier folder objects, not evidence of a continuous earlier organising system.
- **Earlier liked window:** chiefly 2018–2025, with the few 2017 observations disclosed rather than discarded. Creator observations and exported hashtag frequencies provide the coarser multi-year evidence. The 37.98% hashtag coverage is for all retained likes, not every early year; show the actual year/channel coverage. Missing hashtags do not mean no topic. Do not backfill folders onto earlier likes or show one seamless topic timeline.
- **Creator backbone:** all 10,054 retained saved/liked observations remain available to descriptive lifecycle counting, with behaviours separate and combined. Retention is incomplete and uneven; a creator absent here can be central elsewhere. No inferred cross-platform identity linkage.

Collection creation is dated; placements are not. A current membership label means **“in this folder in this snapshot, grouped by save date”**, never “filed then.” Saved times are action times, not publication dates. A 2019 post saved in 2026 is evidence of a 2026 saving action, not a measured 2019 interest or a proven 2026 viewing session. Reference, aspiration, shopping, recipes and memes can all be saved; folders only partly distinguish user intentions in the recent window.

## Creator lifecycle definitions

YouTube's official page describes a trailing 28-day audience and new/casual/regular viewers. We invert viewer-of-channel to creator-observed-by-user, not copy a claim about loyalty. Its page says casual means 1–5 months and regular means more than six, but elsewhere says at least six. **Exactly six months is therefore not consistently specified.** Retain the count explicitly instead of silently assigning it. Source: [YouTube viewer definitions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10246996/understand-returning-and-new-viewers-data).

The following are HINDSIGHT's declared operational conventions, not validated norms:

| Field | Default and user-facing meaning |
| --- | --- |
| First contact | First recorded action for that creator in selected snapshots; never first-ever exposure |
| New-contact flag | First recorded action, or an action after more than 12 calendar months without one here; does not reset original first-recorded date |
| Active-month pattern | At each local month end, count months with at least one recorded action in the trailing 12 calendar months, including that month; current month marked partial |
| Pattern bands | 1–5, exactly 6, 7–12, and no observations in that window; display the actual month count, not a judgement. Technical mapping: casual-like 1–5, regular-like >6, six explicitly unassigned |
| Monthly audience, inverted | Unique known creator IDs with an action in `(asOf - 28 days, asOf]`; label “creators recorded in the last 28 days” |
| Activation convention | Whether another recorded action occurs 7–30 elapsed days after first contact, inclusive. Label “another record 7–30 days later,” not successful activation |
| Retention checkpoints | Another action in elapsed-day bins [7,14), [30,37), [90,97) after first contact; label “recorded in this follow-up window” |
| Inactivity convention | 30/60/90 elapsed days since last recorded action; default detail marker 60. These are configurable elapsed-time markers, not an ended relationship |
| Peak window | Highest distinct active-day count in a trailing 28-day window; tie by earliest window end. Show days and records, never “peak interest” |
| Last recorded | Latest retained action; label “last recorded here,” with snapshot endpoint and days elapsed |

All clocks/windows, channel, snapshot scope and timezone travel with the result and are configurable. Defaults are engineering choices; 7/30/90 is not a universal behavioural law. Use elapsed durations for day windows and calendar arithmetic for months, with end-of-month clamping. A checkpoint extending beyond the snapshot is “not yet observable,” not a negative. At the history's left edge, observed active months are a lower bound, not a complete rolling year. Unknown creator IDs remain an unassigned count. New-contact and active-month pattern are separate fields so a reappearance is not forced into one exclusive label.

Full lifecycle rows apply to repeat creators (at least two retained observations with a known ID in the selected channel). Exactly-one-observation creators remain in aggregate counts; do not fit thousands of singleton curves. Two actions in one sitting are still weak evidence: retain distinct days/weeks/months and session counts rather than call them sustained contact. Ranking uses active months, active weeks, then distinct sessions; raw event count is detail, not the ordering score. Keep saved, liked and combined lifecycles identifiable; they need not agree.

Proposed lifecycle record: `creatorRef, snapshotRefs, channel, observationCount, distinctDays, activeWeeks, firstRecorded, newContactEpisodes[], followUpWindows[], activeMonthsByWindow[], patternByWindow[], peakWindow, lastRecorded, observationCoverage, configRef`. No inferred friend/creator profession or emotional category. Source labels remain untrusted; committed examples are synthetic or ranked, and named detail stays personal.

## Kleinberg burst detection: method and its limits

Use the batched relative-frequency formulation: for a stream and time bin, model relevant records out of all eligible records with state-dependent probabilities, and penalise upward state changes. Dynamic programming selects an intensity-state sequence; elevated runs can form nested intervals. Applying this separately to topics/creators permits overlaps across lanes. It does not label topics, enforce sustained recurrence or guarantee rejection of a singleton. Source: [Kleinberg, Bursty and Hierarchical Structure in Streams (2002)](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/home/kleinber/bhs.pdf).

Proposed HINDSIGHT configuration, fixed before a real run:

1. Use Monday-starting UTC weeks; display local dates explicitly. Recent topic streams are current folder IDs, never an inferred cluster of folder names. One saved observation contributes at most once per folder; multiple folders may include it, so streams are overlapping and not additive. Unmatched/ambiguous placements have no usable save date and are excluded from rate numerators with counts disclosed.
2. For a topic lane, begin after its creation week and within the recent window. The creation week and saves predating creation remain visible as separately qualified records, not zeros before the topic existed. This conservative exclusion avoids inventing historical membership; the resulting lane still describes present-day folder membership, not observed filing activity.
3. Primary `n_t` is all retained saves in an eligible week; `r_t` is distinct matched saved observations currently in that folder. Retain filed/total coverage alongside each week. A prescribed second denominator uses all matched-filed saves, exposing sensitivity to incomplete filing. Neither denominator proves complete topic coverage. Do not turn missing filing into a claim of no topic.
4. Within each contiguous sequence with observed exposure, baseline `p0 = sum(r_t)/sum(n_t)`. Higher states use `p_j = p0 * s^j` only while strictly below 1. Primary `s=2`, maximum three elevated states, upward transition cost `gamma * max(0,j-i) * ln(max(2,T))`, primary `gamma=1`. State cost is `-r_t*ln(p_j) -(n_t-r_t)*ln(1-p_j)`, omitting the state-independent binomial coefficient. Start in state zero; tie-break toward lower intensity then earlier boundary. Baseline 0 or 1 produces no identifiable elevated-rate state, not an invented burst.
5. A zero-exposure week has no rate evidence. Split fitting spans at these gaps and display them unassessed; do not bridge them into a confident bar. Partial endpoint/creation weeks are descriptive only. Singleton exclusion is an **explicit policy**, not a theorem of the algorithm: candidate bars require at least two observations on at least two distinct local dates. Keep excluded records in descriptive counts. These support settings remain visible/configurable and must not be tuned to create pleasing bars.
6. Publish every elevated interval's weeks, action counts, active days/weeks, baseline and observed fractions, state/penalty and edge/coverage limitations. Test the prespecified `s={1.5,2,3}` × `gamma={0.5,1,2}` sensitivity grid, with the same fixed primary, and both denominator choices. Disagreement stays visible. No automatic parameter promotion, life-phase success claim or significance claim follows from a high state.

Creator lanes can apply the same weekly rate method on repeat-creator streams over their available history, with channel-specific denominators and the same gap rules; lifecycles remain the descriptive multi-year backbone whether a burst is present or not. Earlier hashtags remain frequency evidence in this step, not a substitute for labelled historical topics. Sustained-contact ordering is separate from burst intensity; Kleinberg alone does not fix raw-count ranking or a one-sitting recommendation.

No additional runtime dependency is needed for these finite-state calculations. Development tests must cover an all-baseline series, one observation, constant share with changing volume, known overlapping synthetic streams, zero exposure, right censoring, duplicate placements and late-created folders. Measure this method's actual cost before scaling; the earlier synthetic JSD benchmark is not a burst-runtime estimate. Null/control and sensitivity evidence should inform limitations, not generate a score about the user. The discarded changepoint gates do not automatically become this method's success criteria.

## Session threshold: derive when supported; otherwise 60 minutes

Halfaker and colleagues fit mixtures to log inter-activity gaps and recommend about one hour as a useful heuristic across diverse activity logs, while reporting exceptions and unsuitable fits. That supports trying the method, not promising a two-component valley in Instagram saves/likes. The paper does not establish one hour as universal, nor does it validate these action records as viewing sessions. [Halfaker et al., session identification](https://arxiv.org/html/1411.2878).

Prespecified fitting protocol (now implemented; measured results in step-6-validation.md): calculate successive positive timestamp gaps independently for saved, liked and combined streams; retain ties in session counting but exclude zero gaps from the logarithm and disclose them. Plot counts on fixed log10-second bins with component curves and the selected crossing. Keep long gaps, flag their effect and do not delete them to improve the fit.

Fit two Gaussian components to log gaps by deterministic multi-start EM. Proposed numerical settings: means initialised at each of the fixed pairs (1 minute, 1 day), (5 minutes, 12 hours), (30 minutes, 3 days); equal initial weights, initial log10 standard deviations 1, floor 0.1, at most 200 iterations, relative log-likelihood tolerance 1e-6. Retain the best converged likelihood, ordered by mean. Take the unique weighted-density crossing between the means only if the mixture density has two modes separated by a valley. Require at least 100 positive gaps, at least 20 effective observations in each component, and a finite crossing between 1 minute and 24 hours. These are disclosed engineering fit checks, not proof of natural sessions. Inspect the plot before accepting a derived cutoff; no forcing of two peaks or switching to extra components to obtain a preferred answer.

If support, convergence, bimodality or crossing fails, use **60 minutes**, labelled “fallback convention; a stable split was not established.” A manual user override remains labelled as such. Session separation uses a gap strictly greater than the selected threshold; ties and equality remain together. Report the selected/fallback setting and group counts at 15/30/60 minutes plus the derived value if available, as technical method sensitivity, not comparative scores in the memory-aid UI. Do not interpret span as watch time or a group as one decision. The existing exploration's 30-minute tables remain historical results. The separately approved implementation now writes new lifecycle results with reviewed session settings; it does not replace the old exploration.

The precise Catledge/Pitkow derivation and the claim that 30 minutes was “never validated for anything else” are not asserted as established facts here. Prior use is not validation for Instagram, but that does not justify a universal negative claim about all later research.
